# Mybizz-Live — Marketing Plan

The marketing plan of Mybizz for every Mybizz product: Mybizz FDA and Mybizz CS. It sets out what is marketed, to whom, where, when, with which videos and messages, how each video is made, and how results are measured.

Product facts (features, prices, markets) live in each product's concept document, and messages and visual identity live in each product's brand profile. This plan links to them and never restates them.

## 1. Strategy

- **One plan, two separate products.** Mybizz FDA and Mybizz CS are marketed under the one Mybizz brand, each with its own videos, pages and messages.
- **Mybizz FDA is sold first.** Mybizz FDA is the smaller product, complete on its own, and the easier first purchase.
- **Mybizz CS is the next step up.** Mybizz CS launches when it is ready and is offered to Mybizz FDA clients whose business has grown, and to new clients directly.
- **Problem first.** Every piece of marketing starts from the client's problem and shows Mybizz's answer to it. Marketing never tells the history of how Mybizz was built.
- **Personal selling, broadcast proof.** Clients are won by personal one-to-one outreach (teardowns, section 8). Videos, posts and email build the trust and proof that make that outreach work.

---

## 2. Markets and Languages

- **Markets:** the markets in each product's concept document. The United Kingdom is worked first.
- **Languages:** English, French, German, Spanish and Italian, the five app languages.
- **One English master:** every video, page, post and email is made once in English. Each other language is a translation of the English script, never a separate production.
- **Release:** every piece of marketing is released in all five languages.

## 3. Budget

- **Marketing budget: £0.** Every tool in this plan is used on its free tier (section 7).
- **Income buys advertising.** From the month in which Mybizz's income covers its fixed costs, a monthly advertising amount is set in `mybizz-live-budget.xlsx` (section 11). Video production stays free.

### 6.5 How each category is made

| Category | Pictures | Voice | Put together with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Marketing: long form | video_explainer animated scenes; Sarah's intro, mid-video and outro clips from Google Flow | Sarah's voices; five language tracks | OpenShot |
| 2. Marketing: short form | Cut from long-form and explainer videos by video_explainer's Shorts step. A feature clip is a 15-second Remotion animation of one released feature: the feature's real screen, an animated cursor clicking the feature, and its result | Sarah's voices; five language tracks. A feature clip carries on-screen text instead, one version per language | video_explainer |
| 3. Explainer | video_explainer animated scenes built from the approved product documents; Brian's intro and outro clips | Brian's voices; five language tracks | OpenShot |
| 4. Help | Playwright records the real app performing the task, once per language with the app's screens in that language | Brian's Gemini voice; one version per language | FFmpeg, as a batch job |
| 5. Sales: teardown | OBS Studio records the prospect's own website | Sarah's Gemini voice, in the prospect's language | OBS Studio records the screen while the narration plays; OpenShot adds the intro and outro |

**Order of work for a video in categories 1 and 3:**
1. The script is written from the source documents (category 3) or the subject list (category 1).
2. The narration is made with the presenter's Gemini voice, in English first.
3. video_explainer builds and renders the scenes from the script, using the narration as its imported voiceover.
4. The presenter clips come from the library, or a new intro is made in Google Flow.
5. OpenShot joins intro, main part and outro.
6. The four other language narrations are made from the translated script, and FFmpeg produces one audio track per language.
7. The video is uploaded once to YouTube, with all five language tracks.

### 6.6 Video rules

- A help or explainer video says only what the approved product documents say.
- `factcheck` (a video_explainer check against the source document) runs on every explainer video before its final render.
- Each help video is made after its feature has passed its tests.
- A Short reuses the presenter clips of the video it was cut from and never needs new Google Flow clips.
- Every released Mybizz FDA and Mybizz CS feature gets one feature clip, made once the feature has passed its tests. Claude picks the one moment of the feature that films best and writes a 15-second beat sheet for it.
- **Reading time.** Every line of on-screen text stays on screen for at least (number of words ÷ 3 + 0.5) seconds, in every language. A 12-word line needs 4.5 seconds.
- **Square copy.** Every Short is also rendered square (1080 × 1080) for the Instagram and Facebook feeds, from the same project.
- Finished master videos are kept in `G:\My Drive\mybizz-videos\`, one folder per category.

## 8. Teardowns

A teardown is a 60–75 second recording of one prospect's own website. The recording shows where that business's enquiries are lost and how Mybizz FDA answers them; from the Mybizz CS launch month, a teardown may instead show how Mybizz CS answers the gaps it finds. Each prospect receives one teardown, once.

### 8.1 Who receives a teardown

Prospects are scored 0 to 5, one point for each of:

| Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Slow enquiry form | Only a contact form or an email address |
| Phone gap | A mobile number answered by hand |
| Calendar friction | "Email us your availability" instead of real times |
| Small team | One to five people; the owner handles enquiries |
| Active online | Posts, advertises or updates the website |

Prospects scoring 4 or 5 receive a teardown first. Prospects come from LinkedIn searches, Google Maps and business directories in the target markets.

### 8.2 The routine

1. **Choose:** five new prospects each weekday; score them in the teardown tracker.
2. **Record:** one teardown per prospect: the standard teardown intro, Sarah's Gemini voice over the prospect's website in the prospect's language, the standard teardown outro.
3. **Send:** one personal email from `hello@mybizz.live`, signed Mybizz, with the unlisted YouTube link.
4. **Follow up:** one reminder after four days if there is no reply.
5. **Stop:** after that reminder the prospect is never contacted again, and the tracker records the outcome.

The teardown says: "This is a short video from Mybizz, made for [Company Name]." The voice never presents itself as David.

### 8.3 Contact rules

- **Email** goes only to limited companies and limited liability partnerships in the United Kingdom, at a business address. Every email names the sender and gives a one-line way to opt out.
- **Every other prospect** (sole traders, partnerships, and every prospect outside the United Kingdom) receives no unasked email. These prospects are reached through the broadcast channels (videos, posts and the email list), and receive a teardown only after contacting Mybizz first.
- **WhatsApp and SMS** are used only with people who have replied first.
- **Opt-outs:** anyone who asks not to be contacted goes on the tracker's do-not-contact list and is never contacted again.
- **No bulk messages:** every message is written for one prospect.

### 8.4 The teardown tracker

The teardown tracker is a spreadsheet in this folder, `teardown-tracker.xlsx`, with one row per prospect: name, company, country, language, website, contact route, the five scores, total score, date sent, follow-up date, outcome, do-not-contact flag.

## 13. Rules That Never Bend

1. **Honest claims:** marketing claims only the features, channels and prices that are live on the day of publishing.
2. **AI is disclosed:** both presenters are labelled as AI, and no voice presents itself as a real person.
3. **One home per fact:** prices, features and messages are taken from the concept documents and brand profiles at the time each piece is made.
4. **Personal outreach only:** no bulk messages, and the contact rules in section 8.3 apply to every prospect.
5. **Problem first:** every piece of marketing starts from the client's problem.
