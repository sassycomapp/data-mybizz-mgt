Marketing Strategy

The best collective term is **unconventional marketing** (also called **non-traditional marketing**). It covers tactics that depart from standard advertising—such as guerrilla, ambush, ambient, viral, buzz, stealth, and street marketing—to gain attention, engagement, or word-of-mouth with relatively low spend.[[preprints](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202303.0169)][[aak.slu](http://aak.slu.cz/pdfs/aak/2014/03/08.pdf)]

## For your two tactics

- **Sniper marketing** fits under **targeted marketing** or **precision marketing**, because its defining feature is narrowly focusing on a specific audience or account.
- **Guerrilla marketing** fits under **unconventional marketing**, and is frequently treated as an umbrella category that includes ambient, viral, buzz, ambush, and street marketing.[[aak.slu](http://aak.slu.cz/pdfs/aak/2014/03/08.pdf)][[ojs.ikm](https://ojs.ikm.mk/index.php/kij/article/download/3521/3519/6366)]

So, if you need one phrase for a presentation or document: **“unconventional and targeted marketing tactics”** is accurate; if you want a single established label, use **guerrilla marketing** for the creative/public-facing tactics and **precision marketing** for the audience-targeting approach.[[ebsco](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/marketing/guerrilla-marketing/)][[investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/g/guerrilla-marketing.asp)]

## The strategy

Your fastest route to 100 clients is not broad social-media posting—it is a **sniper-first launch**: identify a narrow, high-value niche, build personalised proof and offers for named prospects, then use guerrilla-style attention triggers to make those prospects talk about you. Account-based marketing, interactive demos, referrals, and multichannel outreach are proven SaaS acquisition levers.[[navattic](https://www.navattic.com/blog/b2b-saas-abm-examples)][[cello](https://cello.so/blog/referral-marketing-for-b2b-saas/)][[42dm](https://42dm.net/abm-examples-in-saas/)]

## Positioning first

Do not market “an all-in-one platform.” Market a **specific painful outcome**: “Launch and run a consulting or services business in one day—website, invoicing, email, AI assistant, and client management included.” Your first 100 clients should come from one clearly defined segment, such as independent consultants, agencies, coaches, or small professional-service firms in the US, Canada, UK, and EU.

## Top 10 tactics

### 1. Build a “100 Founding Clients” sniper list

Create a private list of 300–500 ideal prospects: consultants, agencies, coaches, and service-business owners who visibly need a simpler operating stack. Research each company’s current website, booking flow, email setup, and obvious gaps; assign each a tailored reason your platform removes friction.

Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator, industry directories, Facebook groups, YouTube channel owners, and existing communities to build the list. Then run a 30-day sequence: personalised LinkedIn connection, useful value message, short Loom-style demo, WhatsApp Business follow-up where appropriate, and a direct invitation to a 20-minute onboarding call.

**Guerrilla angle:** Call it the **“Founding 100”** and make membership scarce: lifetime pricing, priority onboarding, direct founder access, and a public founding-client badge.

### 2. Create personalised “business teardown” videos

Record 60–90-second personalised videos for high-value prospects: “Here are three things costing your consulting business leads or admin time—and here is how your business could look on our platform.”

Do not sell the software in the first 15 seconds. Lead with a specific observation about *their* business, then show a mock-up of their branded website, booking page, invoice, or automated follow-up sequence built on your platform.

**Sniper angle:** Send the video by LinkedIn DM, email, and WhatsApp. Personalised interactive demos and account-specific campaigns are established high-conversion ABM tactics.[[navattic](https://www.navattic.com/blog/b2b-saas-abm-examples)][[42dm](https://42dm.net/abm-examples-in-saas/)]

### 3. Run “we build it for you” onboarding

Offer a limited-time **Done-With-You Launch Sprint**: for the first 100 clients, your team sets up their website, core pages, email domain, first email sequence, invoice template, and basic AI assistant within 48–72 hours.

This turns your “one-stop shop” promise into an undeniable experience. It also creates a powerful reason to join now rather than “someday.”

**Offer:** “We’ll set up your complete consulting-business operating system in 72 hours—free with your first month, limited to the first 100 businesses.”

### 4. Launch a public build-in-public challenge

Run a 30-day LinkedIn and YouTube series: **“From zero to 100 consulting businesses launched.”** Post daily progress: prospect lists, onboarding calls, client setups, product improvements, wins, mistakes, and metrics.

This makes your launch itself the marketing campaign. It builds trust because prospects see the product, founder, and real customer outcomes rather than polished corporate advertising.

**Content formats:**

- LinkedIn: daily build updates, client wins, before/after screenshots
- YouTube: weekly “how we onboarded a consultant in 48 hours” videos
- Instagram/Facebook Reels: 20–40 second transformations and founder commentary
- WhatsApp: a private “Founding Clients” broadcast channel for updates and early access

### 5. Use “before/after” transformation content

Create side-by-side comparisons: “Consultant’s messy tool stack versus one dashboard,” “manual onboarding versus automated client intake,” or “missed follow-ups versus automated email nurture.”

Make the content specific to consulting and service businesses: proposals, discovery calls, client onboarding, invoices, recurring retainers, lead capture, and follow-up. This is more persuasive than generic feature lists because it shows the buyer’s actual workflow becoming simpler.

**Guerrilla angle:** Publish “The Hidden Cost of Running a Consulting Business on 12 Subscriptions” as a highly shareable LinkedIn carousel, YouTube Short, and downloadable calculator.

### 6. Recruit 20 micro-influencers, not big influencers

Target small but relevant creators: business coaches, freelance consultants, agency owners, no-code educators, productivity creators, and small-business finance creators with 5,000–50,000 engaged followers.

Give them free access, a personalised demo, an affiliate/referral reward, and a ready-made story: “I replaced my patchwork of tools with one platform.” Ask for an honest walkthrough, not a generic ad.

**Sniper angle:** Choose creators whose audience exactly matches your ideal customer profile. Seed 20–30 partners personally before scaling; targeted affiliate recruitment has been shown to generate meaningful referral revenue when supported with materials and onboarding.[[refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/saas-referral-marketing-case-study)]

### 7. Launch a two-sided referral engine

Build referral mechanics directly into the product: every client gets a unique invite link, and both the referrer and new customer receive a meaningful benefit—such as a month credited, an upgraded feature, or a cash commission after the referral converts.

Make sharing effortless inside onboarding, invoices, client portals, email footers, and the dashboard. Referral programmes work best when the reward is native to the product and both sides benefit.[[cello](https://cello.so/blog/referral-marketing-for-b2b-saas/)][[refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/example-viral-marketing)][[trackier](https://trackier.com/referral-marketing-examples-for-marketers-to-cut-cac/)]

**Example offer:** “Invite a fellow consultant: they receive 20% off their first three months; you receive one month free when they activate.”

### 8. Create a “Powered by” visibility loop

Let free or early users generate client-facing assets—proposals, booking pages, invoices, reports, intake forms, or email sequences—with a subtle “Powered by [Your Brand]” mark.

Every deliverable becomes a distribution channel. When a consultant sends a proposal or client portal to a prospect, your brand is exposed to another potential customer in exactly the right context.

**Guerrilla angle:** Make the free tier genuinely useful, but reserve custom domains, advanced automation, or unlimited clients for paid plans. This creates natural product-led virality without feeling spammy.[[linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/garytruitt_5-ways-b2b-saas-companies-can-leverage-viral-activity-7471735579337060352-V1j1)]

### 9. Run a live “Business-in-a-Box” challenge

Host a free five-day challenge on LinkedIn, Facebook, YouTube, and WhatsApp: **“Set up your consulting business operating system in five days.”**

Each day solves one real problem:

1. Define your offer and ideal client
2. Build a lead-capturing website
3. Automate enquiries and bookings
4. Set up invoices, proposals, and client onboarding
5. Launch an email nurture sequence

Participants use your platform during the challenge. At the end, offer a limited founding-client package. This creates activation, social proof, urgency, and a direct path to paid conversion.

### 10. Use guerrilla “tool-stack funeral” campaigns

Create a memorable campaign around the pain your product removes: **“RIP to the 12-tool stack.”** Use bold visual content, mock “obituaries” for disconnected subscriptions, and a calculator showing the monthly cost and admin time wasted across separate tools.

Deploy it as:

- LinkedIn polls and carousels: “How many subscriptions does your consulting business run on?”
- Instagram Reels: “Your business tech stack is costing you R/$X per month”
- YouTube Shorts: rapid-fire “tools you can cancel today”
- Facebook group value posts: a free stack-audit template
- WhatsApp: a one-question audit: “How many tools do you pay for to run your business?”

This is guerrilla marketing in the digital sense: unexpected, provocative, low-cost, and designed to trigger conversation and shares. Guerrilla marketing relies on unconventional, attention-grabbing tactics to create buzz rather than conventional advertising.

## Metrics to manage

Track these weekly, not just followers or likes:

- Qualified prospects contacted and replied
- Demo or onboarding calls booked
- Founding-client signups
- Activation rate: percentage who launch a website, send an invoice, or capture a lead
- Referral invites sent and converted
- Cost per activated customer, not merely cost per lead
- Channel-level conversion: LinkedIn versus YouTube versus WhatsApp versus Facebook

For your goal, I would prioritise #1, #2, #3, #5, #7, and #9. They combine precise targeting, a strong reason to act now, visible proof, and built-in referral growth—the combination most likely to get you to 100 clients quickly.[[navattic](https://www.navattic.com/blog/b2b-saas-abm-examples)][[cello](https://cello.so/blog/referral-marketing-for-b2b-saas/)][[42dm](https://42dm.net/abm-examples-in-saas/)]
