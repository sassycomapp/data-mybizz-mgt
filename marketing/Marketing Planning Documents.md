# Marketing Planning Documents

## Brand & Positioning Documents

- **Branding Profile (Brand Guidelines):** This covers your visual identity (logo, colour palette, typography, UI styling) and your verbal identity (tone of voice, brand values, and personality). For SaaS, this ensures consistency across your marketing website, product interface, and customer support.
- **Positioning & Messaging Framework:** This defines your unique value proposition (UVP). It outlines the exact problem you solve, how your software is different, and provides ready-to-use copy hooks (taglines, elevator pitches, and key benefit statements) for your website and sales outreach.
- **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) & Buyer Personas:** While an ICP defines the exact type of company you target (e.g., UK accounting firms with 10–50 employees), Buyer Personas map out the specific decision-makers within those companies, detailing their daily pain points, goals, and buying triggers.

---

## Market & Competitive Intelligence

**Competitor Analysis Matrix:** A spreadsheet comparing your top 3 to 5 direct and indirect competitors across pricing tiers, core features, target audience, strengths, and weaknesses. This helps you spot gaps in the market and prepare objection-handling scripts.

**Pricing & Packaging Strategy:** SaaS growth relies heavily on how you structure tiers (e.g., Freemium, Free Trial, Starter, Pro, Enterprise). This document outlines your pricing psychology, feature gating, annual vs. monthly discounts, and GBP (£) pricing structure.

---

## Execution & Growth Documents

**Go-To-Market (GTM) Launch Plan:** A tactical timeline covering the 90 days before and after your product launch. It details your beta testing rollout, Product Hunt or UK tech press outreach, initial waitlist campaigns, and early adopter incentives.

**Customer Acquisition & Channel Strategy:** A breakdown of the primary channels you will use to get users (e.g., SEO/Content Marketing, LinkedIn outbound, Google Search Ads, or industry partnerships) along with your target Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for each.

**Content & Editorial Calendar:** A schedule for producing educational content—such as blog posts, comparison pages, case studies, and onboarding emails—mapped to different stages of the buyer journey (Awareness, Consideration, Decision).

**Marketing Funnel & KPI Tracker:** A dashboard or spreadsheet defining your conversion benchmarks at every stage: website visitors, free trial sign-ups, activated users (those who reach the "Aha!" moment in your app), and paid conversions.

## Lean document set

| Working document                   | What it replaces / includes                                                                                            | Practical output                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Customer & Market Brief**     | ICP, buyer personas, competitor analysis, customer interviews, objections, buying triggers                             | One page: who we serve, what they struggle with, what they use today, why they would choose us |
| **2. Positioning & Messaging Kit** | Positioning statement, UVP, taglines, elevator pitch, benefit statements, objection responses, website copy hooks      | A reusable messaging source of truth for site, ads, outreach, and sales                        |
| **3. Offer & Pricing Plan**        | Pricing and packaging strategy, tiers, feature gating, free trial/freemium decision, annual discount, GBP price points | A pricing page draft plus internal pricing rationale                                           |
| **4. GTM & Acquisition Plan**      | GTM launch plan, channel strategy, content calendar, campaign plan, budget, owners, dates                              | A 90-day execution board with weekly priorities                                                |
| **5. Growth Scorecard**            | Funnel KPI tracker, channel CAC, activation, trial-to-paid conversion, retention/expansion metrics                     | One dashboard reviewed weekly or fortnightly                                                   |

This structure mirrors the core GTM questions: who you sell to, why they buy, how they buy, where pipeline comes from, and how you know it is working.tripledart+2

## What to merge

- **Merge ICP + personas + competitor matrix** into the **Customer & Market Brief**. Keep personas to two or three; focus on the economic buyer, champion, and primary user.tripledart+1

- **Merge positioning + messaging** into one living document. It should contain a one-paragraph positioning statement, three differentiators, and a short message hierarchy—not a separate theoretical framework.quackback+1

- **Merge GTM launch plan + channel strategy + content calendar** into the **GTM & Acquisition Plan**. Your launch is simply the first major campaign inside a continuing acquisition system.gtm-labs+1

- **Keep pricing separate.** It affects product, sales, onboarding, margins, and positioning, so it deserves its own concise document.marketerhire+1

- **Turn the KPI tracker into a scorecard.** Add channel-level CAC, activation/time-to-value, trial-to-paid conversion, churn, and expansion—not just top-of-funnel conversion rates.tripledart+1

## What’s missing

## Customer evidence

Before finalising positioning or pricing, capture 10–20 conversations with prospective or target customers. Record the problem, current workaround, desired outcome, objections, and words they actually use. This turns your ICP and messaging from assumptions into evidence.gtm-labs+1

## Sales enablement

Even a self-serve SaaS needs a small sales-enablement set:

- One-page product overview

- Demo script or interactive demo flow

- Objection-handling notes

- Competitor battle cards

- Pricing FAQ

These are especially useful once prospects begin comparing you with established alternatives.tripledart+1

## Operating cadence

Add a simple review rhythm: weekly channel/funnel review, monthly pricing and messaging review, and a 30/60/90-day GTM retrospective. Also define “stop rules”—for example, pause a paid channel if CAC exceeds a set threshold after a defined spend or time period.saas-marketing+1

## Suggested order

1. **Customer & Market Brief** — validate who you serve and what they currently do.

2. **Positioning & Messaging Kit** — decide what you say and why it is credible.

3. **Offer & Pricing Plan** — decide what they pay and how the offer is packaged.

4. **GTM & Acquisition Plan** — decide how you reach, convert, and launch.

5. **Growth Scorecard** — decide what success looks like and when to change course.

This keeps the work commercial and executable: every document should either help you win a customer, price the offer, choose a channel, or make a better decision next week
