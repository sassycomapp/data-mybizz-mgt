# Script — FDA-3 Separate Tools Against One Front Desk

**Task:** 14 in `marketing-plan.xlsx` (Video Plan sheet): Script EN
**Video:** FDA-3, long-form marketing, target 8 minutes, landscape
**Presenter:** Sarah (on-camera intro, Gemini voice narration)
**Aimed at:** owners paying for several tools
**Purpose:** shows the cost and hassle of juggling separate tools
**App screens:** none

---

## Intro (Sarah on camera, Google Flow, 8 s)

An inbox, a booking link, a spreadsheet and phone reminders. That's four jobs, and they're all yours.

## Narration

**Scene 1 — How the front desk usually grows**
*On screen: tools appear one at a time around a small business: an inbox, a scheduling link, a spreadsheet, a notes app.*

Nobody sets out to build a complicated front desk. It just happens, one sensible decision at a time.

You start with email. Then you add a contact form to your website. Someone recommends a scheduling link, so you sign up for one. You keep track of leads in a spreadsheet, or a notes app, or your head. You set reminders on your phone to chase people who went quiet. Maybe you add a chat box to your website.

Each tool solves one small problem. Together, they create a bigger one.

**Scene 2 — You become the link between them**
*On screen: the owner standing in the middle, copying details from one tool to another.*

None of these tools talk to each other properly. The form doesn't create the lead in your spreadsheet. The scheduling link doesn't know which enquiry the booking came from. The reminder on your phone doesn't know the customer already replied.

So you become the link between them. You copy names and email addresses. You check three inboxes. You remember who you promised to call back. You're the glue holding the front desk together, and glue doesn't get much else done.

**Scene 3 — The costs add up quietly**
*On screen: small monthly charges stacking up, each with a team-member icon multiplying it.*

Then there's the money. Each tool has its own subscription. Many charge per person, so every team member you add raises the bill again. Some start free and become paid the moment you need a feature that matters.

Individually, each charge looks small. Added together, and multiplied by your team, they become a real monthly cost, for a front desk that still depends on you to make it work.

**Scene 4 — The hidden cost is time**
*On screen: a clock face filled with small tasks: checking, copying, chasing.*

The bigger cost doesn't show up on a bank statement. It's your time.

Checking each inbox. Copying details. Sending the same answers again. Suggesting times, waiting, suggesting more times. Remembering to follow up. Working out, at the end of the month, how many enquiries actually came in and what happened to them.

Every hour spent running the front desk is an hour not spent on paid work.

**Scene 5 — Things fall through the gaps**
*On screen: an enquiry slipping between two tools and disappearing.*

And when work passes between separate tools, some of it falls through the gaps. An enquiry that came in on the website form while you were watching your email. A customer who went quiet and never got the follow-up. A booking that didn't make it into your notes.

Nobody notices, because there's no single place that would show it.

**Scene 6 — One front desk instead**
*On screen: the separate tools fold into one shape with the Mybizz logo.*

Mybizz FDA, the Mybizz Front Desk Assistant, replaces that patchwork with one front desk.

Your email, your website form, your webchat and your text messages all arrive in one place. Your AI front desk reads each enquiry, records the customer automatically, and drafts a reply in your voice from the facts you've given it. You approve it, and it goes back on the channel it came in on.

**Scene 7 — Booking and follow-up, built in**
*On screen: an enquiry turns into a meeting in the calendar, then a follow-up appears a few days later.*

There's no separate scheduling tool to connect. Your front desk reads your Google Calendar, offers the customer real free times and books the meeting. Confirmations, reminders, reschedules and cancellations are handled, and the lead moves along as they happen.

There's no reminder on your phone either. When a customer goes quiet, your front desk can send one short, polite follow-up on the same conversation, after a delay you choose, and it stops the moment they reply.

**Scene 8 — Every customer, one record**
*On screen: one customer card with the full conversation history across channels.*

Every customer has one record: who they are, what they asked for, where they came from, and every message on every channel. Every lead has a clear stage, from new enquiry to meeting booked, to won or not a fit.

No spreadsheet to keep up to date. No copying between tools.

**Scene 9 — One price, the whole team**
*On screen: one price tag: £45 a month, every team member included.*

And instead of several subscriptions that grow with your team, Mybizz FDA is one fixed price: £45 a month, with every team member included. No contract, no setup fee and no add-ons.

A few costs stay with you, because they're genuinely yours: the AI usage on your own AI account, the text messages on your own phone plan, and storage above the free allowance of your own storage account. You pay those directly, at cost, with no mark-up.

**Scene 10 — A morning with separate tools**
*On screen: a split screen: the owner switching between five windows; the clock moves fast.*

Picture a typical morning. You open your email and find two enquiries. You check the website form, and there's another one from last night. You copy the details into your spreadsheet. You send each person your scheduling link. One of them picks a time; the other asks whether Thursday works instead. You check your calendar, reply again, and set a reminder to chase the third person on Friday.

It's nine forty-five, and you haven't started any paid work.

**Scene 11 — The same morning with one front desk**
*On screen: one tidy list: three enquiries, three drafts, three suggested times.*

Now picture the same morning with one front desk. All three enquiries are already in one list, each customer recorded, each with a draft reply that offers real free times from your calendar.

You read the drafts, approve them, and get on with your day. When the customer picks a time, the meeting is booked. If the third person goes quiet, the follow-up is already arranged. It's nine fifteen, and the front desk is done.

**Scene 12 — Seeing the whole picture**
*On screen: a simple dashboard: enquiries by channel, replies within 5, 15 and 60 minutes, meetings booked.*

Because everything happens in one place, you can finally see the whole picture. How many enquiries came in, and on which channel. How quickly they were answered. How many became meetings, and how many of those meetings actually happened.

With separate tools, that picture would take an afternoon of adding up. With one front desk, it's simply there.

**Scene 13 — Simple, in one place**
*On screen: the one front desk, calm and tidy; the owner back at work.*

Separate tools each do one job and leave you to do the rest. One front desk does the whole job, from the first message to the booked meeting and the follow-up, and shows you what happened.

Never miss a qualified enquiry again.
