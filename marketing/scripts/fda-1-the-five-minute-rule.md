# Script — FDA-1 The Five-Minute Rule

**Presenter:** Sarah (on-camera intro in her Google Flow voice; narration in her narration voice)
**Aimed at:** owners of consulting and service businesses who answer enquiries late
**Purpose:** shows that the first helpful reply wins the client
**App screens:** none

## Intro (Sarah on camera, Google Flow, 8 s)

An enquiry lands while you're busy with a client. By the time you reply, someone else already has.

## Narration

**Scene 1 — The moment that decides the sale**
*On screen: a phone lights up with a new message; a clock starts.*

Every enquiry has a moment that decides it. It isn't the moment you send your proposal, or the moment you agree a price. It's much earlier than that. It's the moment someone reaches out, and waits to hear back.

Picture it from their side. They have a problem they want solved. They've searched, they've found a few businesses that look right, and they've sent the same short message to three or four of them. Then they wait. And the first business that comes back with a helpful, sensible reply usually gets the conversation. The others arrive to find the decision already made.

That is the five-minute rule. It isn't a law, and nobody is holding a stopwatch. It's simply how people behave when they need something: the first good answer wins their attention, and attention is where every sale begins.

**Scene 2 — Why good businesses reply late**
*On screen: an owner in a meeting, a laptop closed, messages stacking up in the background.*

Here's the hard part. The businesses that reply late are rarely careless. Usually they're the opposite. They're busy doing the work: running a session, meeting a client, finishing a project, travelling between appointments.

The person who should answer the enquiry is the same person who is delivering the service. So the enquiry waits. An hour becomes an afternoon. An afternoon becomes the next morning. And by then, the customer has already booked a call with someone who answered first.

It isn't a lack of skill or care. It's a lack of hands.

**Scene 3 — Enquiries arrive everywhere**
*On screen: icons for email, a website form, a chat bubble and a text message, each with a waiting message.*

It gets harder because enquiries don't arrive in one place. One comes in by email. Another through the form on your website. Someone opens the chat on your site in the evening. Someone else sends a text message.

Each channel has its own inbox, and each one needs checking. Miss one, and that enquiry simply sits there. Nobody tells you it's waiting. The customer just quietly moves on.

**Scene 4 — The cost you never see**
*On screen: a line of enquiries; some fade away unanswered.*

The cost of a late reply is invisible. You never see the client you didn't win. There's no invoice for the work that went to someone else. There's just a slightly quieter month, and the feeling that enquiries don't turn into work as often as they should.

For a small business, a handful of lost enquiries a month can be the difference between a busy diary and a worrying one.

**Scene 5 — What a good first reply looks like**
*On screen: a short, friendly reply appears, with three suggested meeting times.*

So what does a good first reply do? It doesn't need to be long. It needs to do three things.

It shows the customer they've been heard, in their own words, about their own problem. It answers the obvious first questions from what the business actually offers. And it makes the next step easy, ideally with real times to talk.

A reply like that, sent quickly, tells the customer something important: this business is organised, and it cares.

**Scene 6 — An AI front desk that replies in your voice**
*On screen: the Mybizz logo; an enquiry flows in, a draft reply appears beside it.*

This is the job Mybizz FDA, the Mybizz Front Desk Assistant, was built for.

It watches your email, your website form, your webchat and your text messages. When an enquiry arrives, your AI front desk reads it, works out what the customer needs, and drafts a reply in your voice, using only the facts you've given it: your services, your saved replies, your answers to common questions.

It replies in the language the customer wrote in. It records the customer, so every message is in one place. And it can offer real free times from your calendar, so the customer can book a call without the usual back-and-forth.

**Scene 7 — Fast, but never out of your control**
*On screen: an approve button; a reply goes out with a tick.*

Speed matters, but so does trust. That's why, out of the box, nothing is sent without your approval. Your AI front desk prepares the reply, and you approve it with one click from your phone, between appointments.

When you're comfortable, you can let it send simple things on its own, like acknowledgements, booking confirmations and reminders. Anything sensitive, such as prices beyond your approved starting prices, proposals or commitments, always waits for a person.

So the reply is ready in moments, and the decision stays with you.

**Scene 8 — When the customer goes quiet**
*On screen: a conversation pauses; a short follow-up is sent a few days later.*

Sometimes the customer reads your reply and goes quiet. Not because they've decided against you. Life just got in the way.

Your AI front desk can send one short, polite follow-up on the same conversation after a delay you choose, and it stops the moment the customer replies or asks not to be contacted. No campaigns. No bulk messages. Just one business following up one person, the way a good receptionist would.

**Scene 9 — Knowing how fast you really are**
*On screen: three simple figures: answered within 5, 15 and 60 minutes.*

Most businesses don't actually know how quickly they reply. Mybizz FDA shows you. You can see how many enquiries were answered within five, fifteen and sixty minutes, how many became meetings, and which channels they came from.

When you can see your speed, you can improve it. And when your speed improves, more enquiries turn into conversations.

**Scene 10 — Out of hours**
*On screen: a dark office; a message arrives at 9 pm; a reply goes out within minutes after a one-tap approval on a phone.*

Plenty of enquiries arrive when you're not at your desk at all. In the evening, when someone finally has time to look for help. At the weekend. Early in the morning, before your first appointment.

A customer who writes at nine in the evening isn't expecting a full proposal. But a short, helpful reply that same evening, with a couple of times to talk, feels very different from silence until Monday. Your front desk prepares that reply as soon as the message arrives, so a quick look at your phone is all it takes to send it.

**Scene 11 — In the customer's own language**
*On screen: the same enquiry arriving in French, German and Spanish; each reply comes back in the same language.*

Customers don't always write in English. Someone in Lyon writes in French. Someone in Munich writes in German. Answering them well usually means a slow reply, or a stiff one.

Your AI front desk replies in the language the customer wrote in, using the same approved facts about your business. The customer feels understood from the first message, and you haven't had to reach for a dictionary.

**Scene 12 — The first helpful reply wins**
*On screen: the enquiry clock stops early; a meeting appears in the calendar.*

The five-minute rule comes down to one idea. The customer is deciding while you're still busy. The first helpful reply usually wins.

You can't be in two places at once. But your front desk can be ready for every enquiry, on every channel, at any hour, while you get on with the work only you can do.

Never miss a qualified enquiry again.
