# Script — CS-4 What Separate Tools Really Cost

**Task:** 53 in `marketing-plan.xlsx` (Video Plan sheet): Script EN
**Video:** CS-4, long-form marketing, target 8 minutes, landscape
**Presenter:** Sarah (on-camera intro, Gemini voice narration)
**Aimed at:** owners counting their monthly software bills
**Purpose:** sets the monthly cost of separate tools against one Mybizz CS price
**App screens:** none

---

## Intro (Sarah on camera, Google Flow, 8 s)

Each tool looks cheap on its own. Add them up for a whole team, and the picture changes.

## Narration

**Scene 1 — Small charges, every month**
*On screen: a bank statement with several small monthly charges highlighted.*

Look at the bank statement of almost any small service business and you'll find them: small monthly charges for software. A website here. A booking system there. A CRM. An email marketing service. An invoicing tool. Perhaps an AI writing assistant.

Each one seems reasonable on its own. That's exactly why they're easy to overlook.

**Scene 2 — Adding up a typical set of tools**
*On screen: six labelled blocks stacking into a tower: website, booking, CRM, email marketing, invoicing, AI writing.*

So let's add them up properly, for a realistic business. A team of six: an owner, a manager, an administrator, one person delivering the service, and two people selling.

To run that business online with separate tools, you'd typically pay for a website with hosting and a blog. An online booking system with reminders and payment at booking. A CRM with a sales pipeline and email marketing. Invoicing with time tracking. And an AI writing assistant.

**Scene 3 — The per-person problem**
*On screen: several blocks growing taller as six team-member icons are added.*

Here's what catches most businesses out. Many of these tools charge per person. Your CRM charges for each seat. Your invoicing charges for each team member. So the bill isn't just the price on the website. It's that price multiplied by your team.

For our team of six, a typical set of separate tools comes to more than £240 a month.

**Scene 4 — And that's not everything**
*On screen: a list of extras still missing: team inbox, voice access, backups, legal pages, reviews, customer portal, accounts, setup.*

And that total still leaves things out.

It doesn't include a shared team inbox. Or voice access to the business. Or backups. Legal pages for your website. Reviews. A customer portal. Accounts. And it doesn't include the time, or the cost, of setting it all up and connecting it together.

Every new team member adds more on top.

**Scene 5 — The cost of the gaps**
*On screen: the owner copying details between two tools; a clock ticking.*

There's another cost that never appears on a statement: the work of joining it all up. Copying a booking into the CRM. Creating an invoice from a payment. Keeping the customer list in step between three systems.

That's paid for in hours, usually the owner's.

**Scene 6 — One price for all of it**
*On screen: the tower of blocks replaced by one block with the Mybizz logo.*

Mybizz CS puts all of it in one platform. Website, booking, payments through your own accounts, CRM, sales pipeline, email on your own domain with a team inbox and email marketing, invoicing and accounts, time tracking, reviews, a customer portal, AI help and voice access.

For one price: £119 a month in the United Kingdom.

**Scene 7 — The whole team included**
*On screen: six team members, then ten, under one unchanged price.*

That price is for the whole business. Every team member is included, each with their own sign-in and email address. Add a person, and the price stays the same.

There's no commission on your bookings or payments. No add-ons. AI, voice, email, accounts and hosting are all in the price. And there's no contract.

**Scene 8 — More than a cheaper bill**
*On screen: a booking flowing into a contact, a payment and an invoice in one line.*

The saving isn't only in the bill. Because everything is one platform, the joining-up work disappears too. A booking creates the contact. A payment records against the customer. The invoice flows into your accounts.

The hours you used to spend being the link between your tools come back to you.

**Scene 9 — What you'll still pay for yourself**
*On screen: a short, honest list of costs outside the price.*

To be clear about it, a few costs sit outside the price, because they belong to you directly. Payment providers charge their own fees on your payments, as they would anywhere. Your domain name is yours to register. And your backups are kept in your own storage account, which is free up to a generous allowance; above that, the storage company bills you directly.

There are no hidden charges from Mybizz.

**Scene 10 — Over a full year**
*On screen: two yearly totals side by side: more than £2,880 for separate tools, £1,428 for Mybizz CS.*

Monthly figures are easy to shrug off, so look at a full year.

More than £240 a month for separate tools comes to more than £2,880 a year, for our team of six, before the extras that aren't included. Mybizz CS at £119 a month comes to £1,428 a year, for everything, for the whole team.

And if you choose the annual plan, Mybizz CS is £1,190 for the year.

**Scene 11 — When the team grows**
*On screen: the separate-tools tower grows with each new hire; the Mybizz price line stays flat.*

The gap widens as you grow. Hire a seventh person, and several of your separate tools charge again, for another seat, another licence, another user. Hire an eighth, and it happens again.

With Mybizz CS, every team member is included. Growth doesn't come with a bigger software bill.

**Scene 12 — The cost of setting it up**
*On screen: a long checklist of setup tasks across several tools.*

There's also the cost of getting started. With separate tools, someone has to choose each one, set it up, and connect it to the others. That's either days of your own time, or money paid to someone who does it for you.

With Mybizz CS, you set up your business once, and everything uses the same information from the start.

**Scene 13 — Comparing fairly**
*On screen: a checklist: everything you need, for your whole team, including setup and extras.*

If you compare prices for yourself, compare like with like. Not one tool against one platform, but everything your business needs, for your whole team, including the extras and the time it takes to keep it all joined up.

That's the comparison that shows what separate tools really cost.

**Scene 14 — Count it up for yourself**
*On screen: a simple calculator; the brand statement appears.*

Take a moment to add up your own tools, for your own team. Then compare it with one platform at one price, with everything connected and your whole team included.

One platform. One price. Your customers. Your data.
