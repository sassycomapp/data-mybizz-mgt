# Script — FDA-2 Approve Before Send

**Task:** 11 in `marketing-plan.xlsx` (Video Plan sheet): Script EN

**Presenter:** Sarah (on-camera intro in her Google Flow voice; narration in her narration voice)

**Purpose:** shows an AI front desk that never replies without the owner's approval
**App screens:** none

## Intro (Sarah on camera, Google Flow, 8 s)

You'd like help answering enquiries. You just don't want a machine making promises in your name.

## Narration

**Scene 1 — The worry is reasonable**
*On screen: a business owner looking at a phone, hesitant.*

If you run a consulting or service business, your reputation is the business. Every reply you send says something about you: how you speak, what you promise, how carefully you think.

So when someone suggests letting AI answer your enquiries, it's reasonable to hesitate. You've probably seen chatbots get things wrong. You've seen confident answers that were simply made up. And you've wondered what would happen if one of those went out under your name, to a customer you really wanted.

That worry isn't old-fashioned. It's good judgement.

**Scene 2 — The real problem is still there**
*On screen: unanswered enquiries piling up while the owner works.*

But the problem that makes AI tempting hasn't gone away. Enquiries arrive while you're busy. They wait. Some of them wait too long, and the customer goes elsewhere.

So the question isn't whether you should let a machine speak for you. The question is whether you can have the speed of an assistant without giving up control of what's said.

**Scene 3 — A different rule: you approve, then it sends**
*On screen: a draft reply with a single "Approve" button.*

Mybizz FDA, the Mybizz Front Desk Assistant, starts from a simple rule: approve before send.

When an enquiry arrives, your AI front desk reads it and prepares a reply. But it doesn't send it. It puts the draft in front of you, together with the customer's message, and waits.

You read it. If it's right, you approve it with one click, from your desk or from your phone. If it needs a change, you edit it and send. If it's not right at all, you reject it and say why.

Nothing leaves your business that you haven't seen. Every new account starts this way.

**Scene 4 — Facts only from you**
*On screen: a folder labelled "Your services, your answers" feeding into the draft.*

The next question is: where do the words come from?

Your AI front desk drafts its replies only from what you've approved: your business profile, your services, your saved replies and your answers to common questions. It never invents a price, a guarantee, a delivery date or advice. If the answer isn't in what you've given it, it doesn't make one up.

That means the draft you approve already sounds like you, and says only what's true about your business.

**Scene 5 — Some things always wait for a person**
*On screen: a short list: prices beyond your starting prices, proposals, commitments, sensitive replies, each with a lock.*

As you get comfortable, you may want some replies to go out on their own. Simple, low-risk messages, like an acknowledgement, a booking confirmation or a reminder.

Mybizz FDA lets you choose that, one type of message at a time. But some things always wait for a person, whatever you set. Prices beyond the starting prices you've approved. Proposals. Contracts. Commitments. Sensitive replies. Those never go out without a team member's approval.

So control isn't a setting you might forget to switch on. It's built into how the front desk works.

**Scene 6 — Honest with your customers**
*On screen: a webchat opening with the line "You're chatting with our AI assistant."*

Approval protects you. Honesty protects your customers.

When a customer opens the chat on your website, the first line tells them they're talking to your business's AI assistant. No pretending to be a person. And if a customer asks to stop receiving messages, every automated message to them stops at once.

People are comfortable with a helpful assistant. What they don't like is being misled. Your front desk is built never to do that.

**Scene 7 — A name on every action**
*On screen: a simple timeline: received, drafted by AI, edited by Anna, approved by Anna, sent.*

Every step is recorded. The customer's message, the AI's draft, any edit a team member made, and the message that was finally sent are each kept as separate records. Nothing is overwritten.

And every approval, edit and send has a name on it: the person who did it, or a note that the AI or the system did it. If a question ever comes up about what was said and by whom, the answer is there.

**Scene 8 — The queue: what needs you today**
*On screen: a short list headed "Needs you today", with approve, edit and snooze.*

All of this comes together in one place: a short list of the things that need you today. New drafts waiting for approval. Customers waiting for a reply. Anything that failed, such as a disconnected channel, so nothing goes wrong silently.

You work through it in minutes, whenever you have a gap. The customer gets a fast, careful reply. You keep the final word.

**Scene 9 — Speed and control, together**
*On screen: an enquiry arrives; a draft appears in seconds; the owner approves; the customer replies "Perfect, thank you."*

That's the balance Mybizz FDA was designed for. The AI does the slow part: reading, sorting, drafting, finding free times in your calendar. You do the part that matters: deciding what your business says.

You don't have to choose between answering quickly and answering well.

**Scene 10 — What a draft looks like**
*On screen: the customer's message on the left, the AI's draft on the right, with the facts it used underneath.*

When you open a draft, you see everything you need to judge it in one place. The customer's message. The details your front desk picked out, such as what they need, how urgent it is, and how to reach them. And the reply it has prepared, written in your tone, using your own answers.

Most of the time, approving takes a few seconds. You're not writing from scratch. You're checking, and occasionally adjusting a word or two.

**Scene 11 — When the AI isn't sure**
*On screen: a draft marked for attention, with a note asking the owner to decide.*

Sometimes an enquiry doesn't fit neatly. It might be a supplier, not a customer. It might ask for something you don't offer. It might be spam.

Your front desk sorts each enquiry by type, and anything unclear is put in front of you to decide. It doesn't guess its way into a promise. And if something goes wrong, such as a channel disconnecting or the AI failing to respond, that problem appears at the top of your list too. Nothing fails quietly.

**Scene 12 — Building confidence at your pace**
*On screen: a slider moving slowly from 'Draft only' to 'Approve before send' to 'Send simple replies automatically'.*

You decide how much help you want, and you can change your mind at any time.

Some owners begin with draft only, where the AI writes and a person sends. Most use approve before send, the starting setting. Later, many choose to let simple, low-risk replies go out on their own, such as confirming a booking or sending a reminder, while everything else still waits for them.

There's no rush. The front desk works the way you're comfortable with.

**Scene 13 — Your voice, your rules**
*On screen: the line "Nothing goes out without your approval until you say so."*

Your AI front desk replies in your voice, books the call and follows up. And nothing goes out without your approval until you say so.
