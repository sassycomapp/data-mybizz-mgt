# Script — CS-2 Your Customers, Your Data

## Intro (Sarah on camera, Google Flow, 8 s)

On a marketplace, the bookings feel like yours. But whose customers are they, really?

## Narration

**Scene 1 — Why marketplaces appeal**
*On screen: a busy marketplace page with many businesses side by side.*

Booking marketplaces are easy to start with. You create a profile, list your services, and customers can find you and book. For a new business, that's appealing. There's no website to build and no booking system to set up.

And for a while, it works. Bookings come in. The diary fills up.

**Scene 2 — The customer belongs to the platform**
*On screen: a customer's details moving into a box labelled with the marketplace's name, not the business's.*

But look a little closer at how it works. The customer found you through the marketplace. They booked through the marketplace. Their details, their history and often their messages live in the marketplace's system.

In practice, your customers are the marketplace's customers. You're allowed to serve them. You're not the one who holds the relationship.

**Scene 3 — Paying for your own customers**
*On screen: a booking with a percentage being taken from it.*

Many marketplaces also take a commission on new customers, and on some platforms that's more than a fifth of the booking. Every time the platform introduces someone, a share of the value goes to the platform.

And your visibility is decided by the platform's rules. Where you appear in the list, how often you're shown, what you're allowed to say. All of it can change, and none of it is up to you.

**Scene 4 — What happens when you leave**
*On screen: the business walking out of the marketplace; the customer list stays behind.*

The real test comes when you want to leave. Perhaps the fees have risen. Perhaps the rules have changed. Perhaps you simply want your own brand.

On a marketplace, when you leave, your customers stay behind. The people you served, the reviews you earned, the history you built: it all belongs to the platform. You start again from nothing.

**Scene 5 — A different idea of ownership**
*On screen: the customer list inside a box labelled with the business's own name.*

Mybizz CS starts from a different idea. Your business, your customers, your data. Nobody else's.

Mybizz CS isn't a marketplace. It doesn't list you beside your competitors. It doesn't take commission on your bookings or payments. And it doesn't keep your customer relationships.

**Scene 6 — Private by design**
*On screen: one separate house per business, each with its own lock.*

Each business on Mybizz CS gets its own private instance, with its own database, on its own domain. Your customer database isn't shared with other businesses, and it isn't pooled into a big shared system.

Your customers book on your website, under your name. Their details go into your database. Their history builds up in your CRM.

**Scene 7 — Backed up to your own storage**
*On screen: a daily backup moving into storage labelled with the business's name.*

Every day, your data is backed up to your own storage account, in your name. Not Mybizz's storage. Yours.

And you can export all of your data whenever you like, without asking anyone.

**Scene 8 — Yours on the first day and the last**
*On screen: a calendar flipping from the first page to the last; the list stays with the business throughout.*

That means your customer list is yours on the first day and on the last.

If you ever decide to leave Mybizz CS, you take it with you. Your customers, your history, your backups. No one else has a copy, and no one can keep it from you.

**Scene 9 — Building something that lasts**
*On screen: a growing customer list turning into repeat bookings and referrals.*

When you own your customer relationships, every customer adds to something that lasts. You can contact them directly, send your own newsletters, invite them back, and ask for reviews on your own website.

Instead of renting access to customers, you're building a business with real value of its own.

**Scene 10 — A tale of two businesses**
*On screen: two identical businesses side by side; after five years one has a growing list, the other an empty box.*

Imagine two businesses offering the same service, in the same town, for five years.

The first takes every booking through a marketplace. After five years, it has served hundreds of people, but if it leaves the platform tomorrow, it has no list to show for it.

The second takes bookings on its own website. After five years, it has a customer list of several hundred people it can contact directly, invite back and tell about something new. Same work. Very different businesses.

**Scene 11 — Bringing customers home**
*On screen: a customer following a link from a marketplace profile to the business's own website.*

You don't have to walk away from a marketplace overnight. Many businesses start by giving every customer a simple reason to book directly next time: a link to their own website, their own booking page, and their own newsletter.

Each customer who books directly is one more relationship that belongs to you. Over time, the balance shifts, and the business stands on its own.

**Scene 12 — Your own email list**
*On screen: a newsletter sent from the business's own domain to its own customers.*

Owning your customer list also means you can talk to your customers yourself. Mybizz CS includes email marketing, sent from your own domain, to your own customers, with their permission.

You can share news, offer a new service, or simply remind people you're there. No platform decides whether your message gets seen.

**Scene 13 — What ownership means**
*On screen: two clear labels: 'Yours: customers, data' and 'Provided: the software'.*

To be precise about it: the software itself is provided by Mybizz, and you use it for a monthly price. What you own is what matters most: your customers and your data.

The platform works for you. It doesn't own anything of yours, and it never sits between you and the people you serve.

**Scene 14 — Trust for your customers too**
*On screen: a customer seeing the business's own name, website and email address at every step.*

Ownership matters to your customers as well. When they book with you, they see your name, your website and your email address at every step. Their details stay with the business they chose, not a platform they've never thought about.

That's a better experience for them, and a clearer relationship for you.

**Scene 15 — Your customers. Your data.**
*On screen: the business's own website, with its own name and booking button, beside the brand statement.*

A marketplace can be a useful way to start. But a business deserves to own its customers.

With Mybizz CS, they're yours from the first booking, and they stay yours.

One platform. One price. Your customers. Your data.
