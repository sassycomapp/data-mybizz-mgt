# Business Planning Documents

## Financial

**Operating Budget:** This tracks your planned expenses (software tools, cloud hosting, marketing, salaries) before and after launch.

**Projected Cash Flow Statement:** For a UK SaaS startup, this is arguably your most important financial document. It tracks when cash actually enters and leaves your bank account, helping you manage your **cash runway** and account for payment delays or annual vs. monthly billing cycles.

**Projected Income Statement (Profit & Loss):** This forecasts your revenue, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS—like server hosting and payment processing fees), and operating expenses over 3 to 5 years to show when the company will become profitable.

**Projected Balance Sheet:** Alongside the Income Statement and Cash Flow, this completes the "three-statement financial model." It tracks your company's assets, liabilities (such as deferred revenue from annual SaaS subscriptions and unpaid taxes), and shareholder equity.

**SaaS Metrics & Unit Economics Sheet:** Standard accounting statements do not tell the whole story for subscription businesses. You should model your **MRR/ARR** (Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue), **Churn Rate**, **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**, and **Lifetime Value (LTV)**.

**Capitalisation Table (Cap Table):** Even if you are a solo founder, create a spreadsheet tracking share ownership, share classes, and any planned equity pools for future hires or investors.

## Presentation

**Business Plan:** This serves as your master strategic document. Keep it focused on the problem you solve, your target customer profile, your go-to-market strategy, and your competitive advantage.

**Pitch Deck (Executive Summary):** Most UK angel investors and VCs will not read a 30-page business plan initially. Condense your business plan into a 10-to-12-slide visual deck for quick sharing.

## Compliance

**UK Data Protection & Compliance Plan:** Since SaaS businesses handle user data, outline your compliance with **UK GDPR** and the Data Protection Act 2018 (including registering with the **ICO**—Information Commissioner's Office, which is a legal requirement for most UK businesses).

**UK Tax & Compliance Schedule:** As a UK Ltd company, you need to plan for **Corporation Tax** (currently 19% to 25% depending on profits), **VAT** (mandatory once turnover hits £90,000, though many B2B SaaS startups register voluntarily earlier to reclaim VAT on setup costs), and **PAYE/National Insurance** if you pay yourself or staff a salary.

## Operational

**Product Roadmap:** A timeline outlining your Minimum Viable Product (MVP) launch, key feature releases, and technical milestones over the next 12 to 18 months.
