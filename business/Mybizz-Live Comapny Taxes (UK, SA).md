# Mybizz-Live Company Taxes

Operating a UK Limited Company (Ltd) as a South African resident introduces compliance requirements across **both tax jurisdictions**. Because the UK company is incorporated in the UK but managed by a founder living in South Africa, both countries' tax authorities (HMRC and SARS) have overlapping claims that require attention.

## 1. United Kingdom Compliance (HMRC & Companies House)

### A. Registrations

- **Corporation Tax:** You must register your company for Corporation Tax with HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) within **3 months** of starting to trade (e.g., selling subscriptions or incurring business expenses).

- **VAT (Value Added Tax):** You must register for UK VAT if your UK taxable turnover exceeds **£90,000** over a rolling 12-month period, or if you make digital SaaS sales directly to UK consumers (B2C) where threshold rules vary.

- **Companies House Accounts:** Registration with Companies House is automatic upon incorporation, but you must maintain annual statutory filings.

### B. Tax Rates

- **Small Profits Rate (19%):** Applies to taxable profits up to £50,000.

- **Marginal Relief (19% – 25%):** Applies on a sliding scale for profits between £50,000 and £250,000.

- **Main Rate (25%):** Applies to taxable profits over £250,000.

### C. Declarations, Deadlines & Payment Dates

- **Corporation Tax Return (Form CT600):** Must be submitted electronically to HMRC within **12 months** following the end of your company's accounting period.

- **Tax Payment Deadline:** UK Corporation Tax payment is due **9 months and 1 day** after the end of your accounting period (e.g., if your accounting year ends March 31, tax is due January 1).

- **Annual Confirmation Statement:** Due at Companies House once per year (confirming directors, addresses, and share capital).

- **Statutory Accounts (iXBRL):** Filed with both Companies House and HMRC within **9 months** of your accounting year-end.

## 2. South Africa Compliance (SARS & Reserve Bank)

Because you are a South African tax resident and managing the UK company from SA, South Africa’s **worldwide tax system** applies to you personally and potentially to the UK entity.

### A. Company Tax Residency & Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) Rules

- **Place of Effective Management (PoEM):** Under SA law, if key managerial and commercial decisions for the UK company are routinely made inside South Africa, SARS may deem the UK Ltd to be a **South African tax resident**, subjecting its worldwide profits to SA Corporate Income Tax (27%).

- **CFC Rules (Section 9D):** Even if the company remains tax resident in the UK, because an SA resident holds >50% of the shares, it qualifies as a **Controlled Foreign Company (CFC)**. Its net income may be imputed directly onto your personal SA tax return unless specific foreign business establishment (FBE) exemptions apply.

### B. Registrations & Reporting

- **Personal Income Tax (ITR12):** You must report any salary, director's fees, or dividends received from the UK Ltd on your annual SA tax return.

- **CFC Return (IT10B):** If the UK company is a CFC, you are required to submit an **IT10B form** alongside your annual personal tax return to declare the entity's global earnings.

- **SARS Transfer Pricing Rules:** If you perform work in SA for your UK entity, transactions between yourself (or an SA sole proprietor setup) and the UK Ltd must be conducted at arm's length.

### C. Extracting Funds to South Africa

- **Dividends Tax:** Dividends paid by the UK company to you as an SA tax resident are subject to SA Foreign Dividends Tax (effective rate up to 20%), though credit is usually granted for any UK withholding taxes paid under the Double Taxation Agreement (DTA).

- **Exchange Control:** Bringing foreign earnings into SA or holding overseas bank balances requires compliance with South African Reserve Bank (SARB) regulations regarding cross-border capital and foreign accounts.

## Double Taxation Agreement (DTA)

The Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) between the UK and South Africa is designed to ensure that income earned across both borders is **not taxed twice at the full rate**.

Instead of eliminating tax liability entirely, the agreement coordinates which country gets primary taxing rights and provides **tax credits** or **rate caps** to prevent double exposure.

## 1. Protection on Corporate Profits

For corporate profits generated by a UK Private Limited Company (Ltd), the DTA establishes clear boundaries based on tax residency and permanent establishment:

- **Primary Corporate Taxing Right:** Under **Article 7 (Business Profits)**, profits generated by a company incorporated in the UK are taxed by the UK tax authority (HMRC) at the standard UK Corporation Tax rates (19% to 25%).

- **Avoiding Double Corporate Tax:** South Africa's Revenue Service (SARS) will not tax the company's baseline operational profits *if* the company remains purely a UK tax resident.

- **The "PoEM" Cautionary Boundary:** If SARS determines that the *Place of Effective Management* (where day-to-day strategic decisions are physically made) is in South Africa, SARS can claim corporate residency under DTA **Article 4 (Tie-Breaker Rules)**. If a dual-residence conflict arises, the tax authorities mutual agreement procedure steps in, or Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules apply.

## 2. Protection on Dividends

When the UK company distributes after-tax profits to a shareholder who is a South African tax resident, both **UK rules** and **DTA rules** interact to protect the funds:

### A. The UK Side (At Source)

- The UK does **not** levy a domestic Dividend Withholding Tax (DWT) on corporate dividends paid to overseas shareholders.

- Thus, dividends leave the UK company and reach the shareholder at **100% of the declared amount** without UK tax withheld at source.

### B. The South African Side (At Destination)

- South Africa taxes foreign dividends received by SA tax residents under its domestic rules (maximum effective rate of **20%**).

- **DTA Dividend Cap (Article 10):** Article 10 of the UK-SA DTA caps foreign withholding taxes on dividends. Because the UK imposes 0% withholding tax domestically, the full distribution enters South Africa, where it is reported on the individual’s SA tax return.

- **Exemption & Credit Protection:** Under South African tax law, foreign dividends received by an individual shareholder are eligible for a partial exemption (reducing the effective tax rate to 20%), or if any tax *was* paid in the UK, SARS grants a **Foreign Tax Credit (Section 6quat)**.

## 3. The "Higher Rate" Rule (How Tax Credits Work)

The core principle of the DTA mechanism is that **you effectively pay tax at the higher of the two countries' applicable rates, but never both combined**.

$$\text{Total Tax Paid} = \max(\text{UK Liability}, \text{SA Liability})$$

### Direct Comparison: Profit to Dividend Flow

```
[UK Ltd Corporate Profit] ──(UK Corp Tax 19-25%)──> [Net After-Tax Dividend] 
                                                             │
                                                    (UK Withholding: 0%)
                                                             │
                                                             ▼
[SA Shareholder Receipt] ──(SARS Foreign Dividend Tax)──> [Final Net Balance]
```

1. **Step 1 (Corporate Level):** The UK company pays **19%–25%** Corporation Tax to HMRC on net profits.

2. **Step 2 (Distribution Level):** The remaining profit is distributed as a dividend with **0%** UK withholding tax.

3. **Step 3 (Personal Level):** The SA shareholder reports the foreign dividend to SARS, paying up to **20%** foreign dividends tax in SA, taking advantage of domestic exemptions or Section 6quat credits where applicable.

If a single South African resident owns 100% of a UK Limited company (Ltd), that UK company is automatically a CFC.

## 1. How Section 9D Works: Income Imputation

Section 9D is an anti-avoidance mechanism designed to prevent SA residents from shifting mobile or passive income to offshore entities to defer or avoid South African tax.

- **The Imputation Rule:** The net income of the UK Ltd is recalculated as if the company were a South African tax resident. That proportional net income is then **imputed (attributed) directly onto your personal SA Income Tax return (ITR12)** in the tax year it is earned—**even if you leave the profits inside the UK company bank account and declare zero dividends**.

- **No Double Tax on Dividends:** If income was previously imputed and taxed in your hands under Section 9D, subsequent actual dividend distributions paid out of those exact profits are generally exempt from South African foreign dividend tax to prevent double taxation.

## 2. Key Exemptions (How to Avoid or Reduce Imputation)

Section 9D contains specific statutory exemptions. If your UK Ltd qualifies for one of these, SARS will not impute the company's net income to your personal return.

### A. The High-Tax Exemption (Most Relevant for a UK Ltd)

- **Rule:** If the foreign tax actually paid by the CFC in its country of residence is at least **67.5%** of the tax it *would have paid* had it been a South African tax resident, the CFC’s net income is fully exempt from imputation.

- **In Practice for the UK:** South Africa’s corporate tax rate is 27%. The 67.5% threshold equates to an effective foreign tax rate of **18.225%** ($27\% \times 67.5\%$).
  
  - Because UK Corporation Tax is currently **19%** (for small profits up to £50,000) or **25%** (for profits over £250,000), a UK Ltd actively paying standard UK Corporation Tax will usually meet or exceed this high-tax threshold.
  
  - *Result:* The net income is exempt from being added to your personal SARS tax return, provided UK taxes are fully assessed and paid.

### B. The Foreign Business Establishment (FBE) Exemption

- **Rule:** Income attributable to a genuine foreign business establishment (a fixed physical place of business outside South Africa that is suitably staffed, equipped, and operated for the company’s primary operations) is exempt.

- **The "Solo Founder / Digital Nomad" Trap:** South African courts (notably confirmed by the Constitutional Court in *Coronation v CSARS*) take a strict view on FBE claims. If a UK Ltd is operated solely from a laptop by a founder residing in South Africa—without local UK employees, physical premises, or operational infrastructure in the UK—it will **fail the FBE test**. However, you would still rely on the High-Tax Exemption above.

## 3. Compliance & Reporting Requirements in SA

Even if your UK Ltd qualifies for an exemption and owes **R0** in additional SA tax under Section 9D, you still have statutory reporting obligations:

1. **IT10B Form Submission:** When filing your annual SA Personal Income Tax return (ITR12), you must complete and submit an **IT10B (Controlled Foreign Company Return)** declaring your ownership percentage, financial statements, and foreign taxes paid.

2. **Transfer Pricing (Section 31):** Any cross-border transactions between you (as an SA resident) and your UK Ltd (e.g., charging developer fees, director fees, or licensing fees to your UK entity) must be conducted on an **arm’s-length basis**.
